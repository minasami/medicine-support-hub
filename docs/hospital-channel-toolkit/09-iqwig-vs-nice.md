# IQWiG vs NICE Methods

Both are influential HTA systems. They answer different questions.

## High-level difference

| Aspect | IQWiG / G-BA (Germany) | NICE (England) |
|--------|------------------------|----------------|
| Primary question | Does the new drug offer **added clinical benefit** vs the appropriate comparator? | Is the new drug **cost-effective** (is the benefit worth the cost)? |
| Main output | Added-benefit rating (six categories, often per patient group) | Recommendation + ICER (£ per QALY) |
| Economics at first stage | Not the core of the early benefit assessment | Central to the decision |
| Timing | Starts at / immediately after launch | Around or after launch; can take longer |

## Method comparison

| Dimension | IQWiG | NICE |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Evidence preference | Strong preference for direct RCTs vs defined comparator | Broader evidence accepted when justified |
| Surrogate endpoints | Strict; prefer hard clinical outcomes | More accepting if reasonably likely to predict benefit |
| Modelling / extrapolation | Generally not used for the benefit rating itself | Expected (lifetime models, sensitivity analyses) |
| Comparator | G-BA defines the appropriate comparative therapy (zVT) | Usually NHS standard of care |
| Uncertainty | Certainty grades: proof / indication / hint | Sensitivity analyses; managed access possible |
| Orphans / rare disease | Simplified path initially; full assessment if turnover exceeds threshold | Separate Highly Specialised Technologies pathway |

## IQWiG added-benefit categories (simplified)

- Major
- Considerable
- Minor
- Non-quantifiable
- No added benefit proven
- Lesser benefit

“No added benefit proven” is common. “Major” is rare. The rating strongly shapes later price negotiation.

## Why this matters in Egypt

Egypt’s emerging approach (UPA / UHIA pharmacoeconomic recommendations) sits between the two:
- values clinical evidence and budget impact
- does not apply a rigid NICE-style QALY threshold
- does not use a full German-style added-benefit grading for most hospital products

When a stakeholder says “NICE” or “German HTA”:
- NICE → value for money (cost per QALY)
- IQWiG → clinical added benefit vs standard of care
- Then bring the conversation back to Egyptian realities: budget impact, supply reliability, localization, and account-level formulary process.
