# Formulary & P&T Decision Drivers

Understanding what actually moves a product from “presented” to “listed and used” is essential for hospital-line KAMs.

## Primary Drivers in Egyptian Hospital Accounts

### 1. Clinical Need & Therapeutic Fit
- Does the product fill a clear gap or improve outcomes vs current standard?
- Alignment with existing or emerging treatment protocols.
- Specialist / Key Opinion Leader support (especially for specialty and complex products).

### 2. Quality & Regulatory Credibility
- Registration status and dossier quality.
- Manufacturing standards (local GMP, international accreditations where available).
- Consistency of product quality and documentation.
- Track-and-trace readiness.

### 3. Supply Reliability
- Ability to guarantee continuous availability.
- Warehouse and distribution performance.
- Response to stock-out risk (critical for hospital formularies).

### 4. Economic Considerations
- Acquisition cost / tender price.
- Total cost of therapy (including administration, monitoring, length of stay impact where relevant).
- Budget impact for the hospital or group.
- For public-linked accounts: alignment with UPA / UHIA processes and National Essential Medicines List considerations.

### 5. Stakeholder Relationships & Trust
- Long-term relationship quality with pharmacy, medical, and procurement stakeholders.
- Responsiveness and professional support (medical information, training, complaint handling).
- History of partnership behavior during shortages or crises.

### 6. Ease of Use & Operational Fit
- Compatibility with hospital workflows, protocols, and (increasingly) digital systems.
- Packaging, presentation, and administration practicality.
- Training requirements for nursing / pharmacy staff.

## Typical Decision Layers

| Layer | Typical Stakeholders | Focus |
|-------|----------------------|-------|
| Central Group | Group procurement, central P&T, medical directors | Standardization, cost, group contracts |
| Hospital Level | Chief pharmacist, medical directors, specialty heads | Clinical fit, local protocols, daily operations |
| Specialty / Department | KOLs, department heads | Efficacy, safety, patient outcomes |
| Operational | Ward pharmacists, nursing | Usability, availability, training |

**Practical note:** Winning at one layer does not automatically secure the others. Successful KAMs map and engage all relevant layers.

## How to Prepare for a Formulary / P&T Discussion
1. Clarify the exact decision process and timeline for that account.
2. Identify the primary clinical champion and the economic / procurement decision-maker.
3. Prepare a concise value story covering: clinical rationale → quality/supply → economic framing.
4. Anticipate objections (price, existing alternatives, budget constraints, familiarity with competitor).
5. Have supporting materials ready (summary evidence, supply commitments, training plan).
