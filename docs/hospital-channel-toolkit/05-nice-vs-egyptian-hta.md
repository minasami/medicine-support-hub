# NICE vs Egyptian HTA Approaches

Understanding both systems helps KAMs participate in more sophisticated conversations, especially when stakeholders reference international benchmarks.

## NICE (England) – Technology Appraisal Process (Simplified)

NICE evaluates technologies primarily through:

- **Single Technology Appraisal (STA)** – one product, one indication
- **Multiple Technology Appraisal (MTA)** – broader comparisons
- **Highly Specialised Technologies (HST)** – very rare diseases

**Typical flow:**
1. Company submission (clinical + economic evidence)
2. Evidence Review Group (ERG) critique
3. Appraisal Committee discussion
4. Appraisal Consultation Document (ACD) → Final Appraisal Determination (FAD)

**Key feature:** Explicit cost-effectiveness thresholds (commonly referenced around £20,000–£30,000 per QALY, with flexibility for certain conditions).

NICE decisions strongly influence NHS funding and have international reputational weight.

## Egyptian Approach (UPA / UHIA / EDA)

Egypt is building more structured economic evaluation capacity, particularly for innovative and high-budget products.

**Key characteristics:**
- Joint interest between UPA (procurement) and UHIA (reimbursement)
- National recommendations for pharmacoeconomic evaluation reporting exist
- Focus on practical resource allocation, budget impact, and clinical need
- Less reliance on a single rigid cost-per-QALY threshold than NICE
- Strong influence of national drug policy, Essential Medicines List, localization goals, and supply security
- Pricing remains a regulated process under EDA

**Current reality:**  
Formal HTA is more developed for selected high-cost / innovative categories. For the majority of hospital-line products, decisions still combine clinical judgment, price/tender performance, quality, supply reliability, and stakeholder relationships.

## Comparison Snapshot

| Aspect | NICE | Egypt (current trajectory) |
|--------|------|---------------------------|
| Primary focus | Cost-effectiveness (QALYs) + clinical | Clinical need + budget impact + supply + policy goals |
| Threshold | Explicit (approx. £20–30k/QALY) | Not rigidly applied in the same way |
| Main bodies | NICE | UPA, UHIA, EDA |
| Process maturity | Highly formalized | Developing / selective |
| Localization & supply | Secondary | High priority |
| Private hospital autonomy | Limited (NHS-driven) | Significant for private groups |

## How to Use This Knowledge
- When a stakeholder mentions “NICE” or international HTA, you can acknowledge the methodology while bringing the conversation back to Egyptian realities (budget impact, local evidence, supply, UHI/UPA context).
- Demonstrate professionalism by understanding both systems without claiming that Egyptian decisions must follow NICE thresholds.
