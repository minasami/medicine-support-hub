# Pharmacy Chains, PHI, Insurers & TPAs – KAM Playbook

Hospital groups are only one key-account layer. In Egypt, listing and volume also depend on **pharmacy chains**, **private health insurance (PHI)**, **insurance companies**, and **TPAs** (third-party administrators).

Seed profiles: `docs/data/egypt-kam-key-accounts.seed.json`

## Why these accounts matter

| Account type | What the KAM is really trying to win |
|--------------|--------------------------------------|
| Pharmacy chain | Listing, shelf/availability, chain protocol, central purchasing, reimbursement at the counter |
| Insurance company (payer) | Inclusion in medical policy / formulary of covered medicines |
| TPA | Network listing, approval rules, chronic protocols, pharmacy panel |
| PHI program | Specific product list inside a branded medical plan |
| UHIA (public) | Benefit-package and reimbursement pathway (not PHI, but shapes private behaviour) |

A hospital listing without chain availability or payer coverage often fails at the patient’s last step.

## Decision layers (typical)

### Pharmacy chains
- Central commercial / purchasing
- Category or pharmacy operations manager
- Regional supervisors
- Branch pharmacists (adoption and stock discipline)

### Insurers
- Medical director / chief medical officer
- Provider network / contracting
- Actuarial / product
- Claims and chronic-care unit

### TPAs
- Medical network management
- Pre-authorization / chronic protocols
- Pharmacy benefits / panel management
- Client (insurer or company) account manager

Always separate **who designs the benefit** (insurer) from **who runs the network and approvals** (TPA). They are often different companies.

## What to capture on each account
- Legal name and brand the field team actually hears
- Type: chain / insurer / TPA / PHI program
- Geography and channel (retail, corporate, hospital panel)
- How decisions are made (central vs branch / medical vs commercial)
- Public hotline or website only — no private price lists
- Open field note: “What should a colleague know before the next visit?”

## Talking points by account type

**Chain:** availability, expiry management, patient counselling support, and whether the SKU is reimbursed by major payers.

**Insurer:** clinical need, budget impact on the medical plan, substitution rules, chronic vs acute use.

**TPA:** approval pathway, ICD / protocol fit, network pharmacy behaviour, turnaround time for pre-auth.

**Do not** put confidential tender prices, patient data, or unofficial discount grids in shared notes.

## Suggested visit sequence
1. Map whether the product is used in hospitals, retail, or both.
2. Identify the payer that actually pays (UHIA vs PHI vs cash).
3. Identify the TPA that runs approvals, if any.
4. Identify the chains where insured and cash patients actually collect.
5. Align hospital listing + payer rule + chain availability in one account plan.
