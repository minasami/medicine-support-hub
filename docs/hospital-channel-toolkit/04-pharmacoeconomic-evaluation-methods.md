# Pharmacoeconomic Evaluation Methods – Field Guide

These methods help quantify the value of a medicine. KAMs do not need to run full models, but understanding the language and logic is valuable when discussing high-cost or competitive products.

## Core Methods

### 1. Cost-Minimization Analysis (CMA)
Used when two interventions have **equivalent** clinical outcomes.  
Focus: Which option costs less overall?

**When useful:** Generics vs brands with proven therapeutic equivalence, or biosimilars with strong evidence of similarity.

### 2. Cost-Effectiveness Analysis (CEA)
Compares cost per unit of clinical effect (e.g., cost per life-year gained, cost per event avoided).

**Key metric:** Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER)

$$
\text{ICER} = \frac{\text{Cost}_A - \text{Cost}_B}{\text{Effect}_A - \text{Effect}_B}
$$

### 3. Cost-Utility Analysis (CUA)
A form of CEA that uses **quality-adjusted life years (QALYs)** as the outcome.  
Widely used by NICE and many HTA bodies.

**QALY** combines length and quality of life into a single measure.

### 4. Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)
Both costs and benefits are expressed in monetary terms. Less commonly used in pharmaceutical HTA than CEA/CUA.

### 5. Budget Impact Analysis (BIA)
Estimates the financial impact of adopting a new intervention on a specific budget holder (hospital, payer, health system) over a short time horizon (usually 1–5 years).

**Highly relevant** for Egyptian institutional and UPA/UHIA discussions.

## Egyptian Context Notes
- National recommendations for pharmacoeconomic evaluations emphasize transparency, appropriate comparators, sensitivity analysis, and budget impact.
- Perspective is typically that of the payer / health system.
- Discount rates in the range of 3–5% are commonly referenced in regional and national guidance.
- Formal thresholds (like NICE’s £20k–£30k per QALY) are not rigidly applied in the same way in Egypt; decisions remain multi-factorial.

## Practical Advice for KAMs
- For most day-to-day hospital discussions, focus on **clinical differentiation + total cost of therapy + supply reliability**.
- When the product is premium-priced or innovative, be prepared to discuss budget impact and value in simple terms.
- Never over-claim economic superiority without solid supporting data.
- Use economic language to open constructive dialogue, not to replace clinical relationships.
