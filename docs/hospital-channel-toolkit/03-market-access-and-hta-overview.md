# Market Access & HTA Overview (Egypt Focus)

## What “Market Access” Means for Hospital KAMs
Market Access is the set of activities that ensure patients can obtain and physicians can prescribe a medicine under sustainable conditions (availability, affordability, and appropriate use).

In the Egyptian hospital channel this typically involves:
- Formulary listing and protocol inclusion
- Procurement / tender success (especially via UPA for public volume)
- Reimbursement or benefit package inclusion under UHIA where relevant
- Maintaining supply and stakeholder support after listing

## Key Egyptian Institutions

| Institution | Role relevant to Access |
|-------------|-------------------------|
| **UPA** (Unified Procurement Authority) | Central procurement for public sector entities. Major influence on public hospital volumes and pricing benchmarks. |
| **UHIA** (Universal Health Insurance Authority) | Defines benefit packages and reimbursement lists. Expanding coverage increases its importance. |
| **EDA** (Egyptian Drug Authority) | Registration, pricing, quality oversight, national formularies and drug policy. |
| Private Hospital Groups | Independent formulary and procurement decisions, increasingly professionalized. |

## Practical Market Access Framework for Field Teams

A simple usable model:

**1. Access**  
Can the product be prescribed and dispensed in the target accounts?  
(Formulary status, protocol inclusion, stock availability)

**2. Adoption**  
Are physicians actually using it appropriately and consistently?  
(Education, KOL support, real-world experience, competitive positioning)

**3. Advocacy / Sustainability**  
Is there ongoing support from key stakeholders and a sustainable economic rationale?  
(Relationships, evidence of value, supply reliability, budget management)

## Health Technology Assessment (HTA) in Egypt
Egypt is progressively strengthening the use of economic evaluation and HTA principles, particularly for innovative and high-budget products.

- UPA and UHIA have developed joint approaches to support procurement and reimbursement decisions.
- National recommendations for pharmacoeconomic evaluation reporting exist (focused on innovative / high-budget pharmaceuticals).
- Use of formal HTA is still evolving and is more structured for certain high-cost categories than for the average hospital product.

**For most hospital-line products**, the practical requirements remain closer to:
- Clear clinical positioning
- Competitive pricing / tender performance
- Reliable supply
- Stakeholder trust

than to full formal HTA dossiers. However, the ability to discuss value and budget impact professionally is increasingly expected.
